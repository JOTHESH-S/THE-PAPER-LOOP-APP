School campuses throw away huge amounts of written notebook scrap every day. Left in landfills, it releases methane as it breaks down; burned, it releases CO₂ and toxic ash. The Paper Loop de-inks and re-pulps that scrap using only warm water and baking soda — no chlorine bleach — then strengthens it with a natural cornstarch binder so the recycled sheets are smooth, tear-resistant, and ready to write on again. 📓15–20 written notebook sheets 🧂Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) 🌽Cornstarch (natural binder) 💧Warm & ambient water 🌼Turmeric, beetroot, mustard/marigold seeds 🔧Mixer-grinder, churner, mesh frame, rolling pin Step by Step ## How We Made It Eight simple stages take written waste paper from classroom scrap to a finished, writable sheet — using only household tools and no synthetic chemicals. IMG_1 1 ### Collect & Sort Gather written notebook scrap and rough sheets from the classroom instead of binning them. IMG_2 2 ### Tear into Scraps Hand-tear sheets into roughly 2 cm pieces to expose fiber edges for faster soaking. IMG_3 3 ### Soak & Churn Submerge scraps in warm water and agitate with a wooden churner (kavvam) for 60 minutes. IMG_4 4 ### Alkaline De-Inking Add 1.5 tsp sodium bicarbonate — the mild alkaline bath loosens ink pigments without bleach. IMG_5 5 ### Blend into Pulp Drain the ink water, then blend the de-inked shreds for 30 seconds into a uniform fiber mush. IMG_6 6 ### Split the Batches Divide the pulp into Batch 1 (plain) and Batch 2, which gets the starch binder. IMG_7 7 ### Add Starch Binder Cook 1 tsp cornstarch in 3 tbsp water until translucent, then blend into Batch 2. IMG_8 8 ### Cast, Press & Dry Pour onto a mesh frame, press under cotton with a rolling pin, iron lightly, then fan-dry. Proof It Works ## Starch Binder vs. Plain Pulp Standardized 3 cm × 10 cm strips were loaded with deadweights until they tore, confirming the starch gel bridges microscopic fiber gaps. Batch 1 — Plain Pulp145 g Batch 2 — Starch Binder390 g 168% stronger tensile load capacity - ✓ Zero capillary ink bleed on gel and ballpoint pens - ✓ Smooth, compact, sealed surface instead of rough and porous - ✓ Folds cleanly without snapping — Batch 1 cracks when creased - ✓ Resists a water drop for 40–55 seconds vs. 3–5 seconds - ✓ No chlorine bleach — de-inking done with baking soda alone Impact ## Benefits of The Paper Loop A single school section generates about 1.5 kg of waste paper a month — campus-wide, this process can divert up to 45 kg monthly from dumpsites. 🚯 ### Diverts Landfill Waste Keeps written scrap out of landfills and incinerators, cutting methane and CO₂ emissions. 🧪 ### Zero Toxic Chemicals Uses baking soda for de-inking instead of chlorine bleach — safe for students to handle. 🏠 ### Decentralized No industrial plant or transport needed — runs on a classroom table with household tools. 💰 ### Zero Cost Materials Supplies school events with free, sustainable stationery made from scrap already on campus. 🌾 ### Natural Ingredients Cornstarch, turmeric and beetroot replace synthetic binders and dyes entirely. 📚 ### Teaches Circular Economy Gives students a hands-on model of reduce–reuse–retrieve–redesign–recycle in action. Beyond the Sheet ## What You Can Make With It The starch-bound paper is strong enough to be cut, layered and finished into real campus products. 🌱 ### Plantable Seed Paper Mustard or marigold seeds embedded before drying — plant the bookmark or badge after use. 🎨 ### Naturally Dyed Sheets Turmeric gives a golden finish, beetroot extract gives a pastel rose — no synthetic dye. 🏆 ### Merit Certificates Layered starch-bound sheets form rigid cardstock for school and Eco-Club awards. 🔑 ### Upcycled Keychains Rigid cut-outs with punched eyelets and jute lanyards for school events. 💌 ### Greeting Cards Writable, textured cardstock for artistic and commemorative cards. Final result The Result ## Two Batches, One Clear Winner Fully dried, cured sheets from both batches side by side. Batch 2 — reinforced with the cornstarch binder — came out smoother, stronger and completely writable, proving the hypothesis that a natural polysaccharide gel can replace the strength virgin paper normally gets from long, unbroken cellulose fibers. **The Paper Loop** — S.
