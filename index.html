The Rationale

Why The Paper Loop?

School campuses throw away huge amounts of written notebook scrap every day. Left in landfills, it releases methane as it breaks down; burned, it releases CO₂ and toxic ash. The Paper Loop de-inks and re-pulps scrap using only warm water and baking soda — no chlorine bleach — then strengthens it with a natural cornstarch binder so the recycled sheets are smooth, tear-resistant, and ready to write on again.

Required Materials & Household Tools
📓 15–20 written notebook sheets
🧂 Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate)
🌽 Cornstarch (natural sol-gel binder)
💧 Warm & ambient water
🌼 Turmeric, beetroot & seeds
🔧 Mixer-grinder, churner & mesh frame
Step by Step

How We Made It

Eight stages taking written waste paper to a finished writable sheet without synthetic chemicals.

1
Collect & Sort
Gather written notebook scrap and rough sheets from the classroom instead of binning them.
2
Tear into Scraps
Hand-tear sheets into roughly 2 cm pieces to expose fiber edges for faster hydration.
3
Soak & Churn
Submerge scraps in warm water and agitate with a wooden churner (kavvam) for 60 minutes.
4
Alkaline De-Inking
Add 1.5 tsp sodium bicarbonate — the mild alkaline bath loosens ink pigments safely without bleach.
5
Blend into Pulp
Drain ink wastewater, then pulse de-inked shreds for 30 seconds into a uniform fiber mush.
6
Split the Batches
Divide pulp into Batch 1 (control plain pulp) and Batch 2 (which receives the starch binder).
7
Add Starch Binder
Cook 1 tsp cornstarch in 3 tbsp water until translucent, then blend into Batch 2 to bridge fiber gaps.
8
Cast, Press & Dry
Cast onto mesh frame, press under cotton with rolling pin, iron lightly, then fan-dry until cured.

Impact & Sustainability

Benefits of The Paper Loop

One school section generates ~1.5 kg of paper waste monthly — up to 45 kg diverted campus-wide.

🚯
Diverts Landfill Waste
Prevents paper scrap from entering dumpsites and incinerators, mitigating methane and CO₂ release.
🧪
Zero Toxic Chemicals
Employs mild sodium bicarbonate for de-inking instead of hazardous chlorine bleaches.
🏠
Decentralized Production
No external plant or freight transit required; completely viable in a classroom setting.
💰
Zero Material Cost
Produces campus stationery from scrap already generated within the school ecosystem.
🌾
Organic Ingredients
Cornstarch, turmeric, and beetroot extracts completely replace synthetic glues and chemical dyes.
📚
Circular Economy Model
Offers students a practical demonstration of Reduce, Reuse, Retrieve, Redesign, and Recycle.

Final Conclusion

Two Batches, One Clear Winner

Batch 2 — reinforced with the cooked starch sol-gel matrix — produced a significantly smoother, stronger, and writable sheet. This confirms our core hypothesis: an organic polysaccharide gel effectively bridges microscopic voids between shortened, mechanically recycled fibers, compensating for the tensile loss typically caused by repeated repulping.
